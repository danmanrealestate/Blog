What Small Business Owners Should Know Before Leasing Commercial Space

By Dan Marovich, RE/MAX Ace Realty · September 04, 2026 · 4 min read
Share on Facebook Share on LinkedIn Share on X

Discover essential insights for small business owners leasing commercial space in Southeastern Pennsylvania’s competitive market.

Leasing commercial space is a significant step for small business owners. Whether you're starting a new venture or expanding an existing one, the process requires careful planning and knowledge. In Southeastern Pennsylvania—covering Downingtown, Chester County, Montgomery County, Berks County, Lancaster County, Bucks County, and Delaware County—understanding the local market nuances can make all the difference.

Understanding Your Business Needs

Before signing any lease agreement, take time to evaluate your business needs thoroughly:

  • Space Requirements: Determine how much space is necessary now and consider future growth. Is some flexibility included in the lease to accommodate expansion?
  • Location: Proximity to customers, suppliers, and employees impacts daily operations. A strategic location in areas like Downingtown or Montgomery County can boost foot traffic and accessibility.
  • Budget Constraints: Calculate not just rent but also additional expenses such as utilities, maintenance, insurance, and taxes.
  • Type of Property: Retail, office, industrial, or mixed-use? Ensure the property suits the nature of your business.

Key Lease Terms to Review Carefully

Commercial leases can be complex. Here are crucial terms to understand and negotiate:

  • Lease Duration: Typical leases range from 3 to 10 years. Long-term leases offer stability, while short-term leases provide flexibility.
  • Rent Structure: Understand base rent, common area maintenance (CAM) charges, and any percentage rent agreements.
  • Renewal Options: Securing options to renew the lease can protect your business’s location in the long run.
  • Maintenance Responsibilities: Clarify who handles repairs and upkeep—tenant, landlord, or shared.
  • Subleasing and Assignment: Flexibility to sublease or assign the lease can be vital if your business needs change.

Legal and Regulatory Considerations

Understanding legal and compliance issues is essential:

  • Zoning and Permits: Verify that the space is properly zoned for your business activities and that you have necessary permits.
  • Accessibility Requirements: Ensure the property complies with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) if applicable.
  • Environmental Issues: Older buildings may have concerns such as asbestos or lead paint—that could affect use or renovation.

Financial Planning and Risk Management

Consider your financial strategy in leasing:

  • Security Deposits and Personal Guarantees: Be prepared for upfront costs and possible personal financial liability.
  • Insurance: Ensure adequate coverage for liability, property damage, and business interruption.
  • Exit Strategy: Plan for lease termination or relocation if business needs evolve.

Local Market Perspective

The commercial real estate market across Southeastern Pennsylvania is unique. Areas such as Chester County and Bucks County offer a mix of suburban growth and well-established business districts. Downingtown provides a robust community infrastructure, attracting small businesses seeking visibility and accessibility. Meanwhile, Berks and Lancaster counties are seeing rising interest for light industrial and office spaces due to competitive pricing. Montgomery and Delaware counties continue to be hotspots for retail and service businesses, driven by dense populations.

Key Takeaways for Small Business Leasing

  • Assess current and future space needs to avoid costly relocation.
  • Understand all terms in the lease agreement—don’t hesitate to negotiate.
  • Ensure compliance with zoning, permits, and accessibility laws to avoid costly delays.
  • Factor in all financial obligations beyond rent, including deposits, insurance, and maintenance.
  • Research local market trends to select an optimal location in Southeastern Pennsylvania.

Questions to Ask Before You Move Forward

  • What is the total monthly cost including rent, utilities, and CAM fees?
  • Are there options to renew the lease or expand the space?
  • Who is responsible for repairs and maintenance of the property?
  • Is the space compliant with zoning laws and ADA requirements for my business type?
  • What are the penalties or conditions for early lease termination?

Frequently Asked Questions

What factors should I consider when choosing a commercial space for my small business?

You should consider your space requirements, location accessibility, budget, zoning compliance, lease terms, and future expansion plans to ensure the space aligns with your business needs.

How can I negotiate better lease terms?

Work with a knowledgeable real estate professional, understand standard lease clauses, seek flexibility in rent structures, request renewal options, and clarify maintenance responsibilities.

Are there specific permits needed for commercial leases in Southeastern Pennsylvania?

Yes, depending on your business type, you may need zoning approvals and permits from local municipalities. Always verify these before signing a lease.

What are common hidden costs in commercial leases?

Beyond base rent, tenants should expect expenses like common area maintenance charges, property taxes, utilities, insurance, and possible repair fees.
