Lesson 44 — How to read a commercial rent roll. A rent roll is your fastest snapshot of property income and near-term risk. Don’t treat it as mere paperwork — use it to confirm actual cash flow, upcoming expirations, and tenant obligations before you underwrite or negotiate. Key checks: verify lease dates against signed leases; match rent amounts to leases and payment history to catch concessions or step rents; investigate any units marked vacant and estimate re-lease timing and improvement costs. For local investors and landlords in Chester and Montgomery Counties: pay special attention to parking, municipal service obligations, shared maintenance charges, and tenant mix in mixed-use assets — these drive re-leasing and operating expense surprises.
