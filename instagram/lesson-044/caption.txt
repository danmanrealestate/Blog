Lesson 44 — How to Read a Commercial Rent Roll A rent roll is more than a tenant list; it’s a map of near-term cash flow, vacancy risk, and hidden expense shifts. When evaluating a property, verify tenant legal names against executed leases and COIs, review lease start/end dates plus renewal options, and request a month-by-month payment ledger to spot recurring late payments or write-offs. Pay attention to expense structures (gross, modified gross, or NNN), CAM recoveries, and any ambiguous language that could transfer maintenance, tax, or insurance costs to the owner. Check for clustered lease expirations that could create vacancy waves and review guarantors, subleases, and amendments to resolve apparent discrepancies. For Chester and Montgomery County investors and landlords, prioritize tenant-type fit with local demand and staggered expirations to protect NOI.
