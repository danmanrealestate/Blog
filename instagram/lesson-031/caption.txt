Lesson 31: How to evaluate a small industrial or flex property — a practical checklist for newer investors, landlords, and business owners in Chester and Montgomery Counties. Start by defining the building’s role: office-to-warehouse ratio, clear height, column spacing, and permitted zoning. On-site checks should include truck turning radius, curb cuts, bay door heights, employee parking, and local street weight limits. Inspect electrical capacity, HVAC zoning, roof condition, and fire suppression; estimate upgrade costs and realistic timelines. Review leases for permitted uses, hazardous activities, and missing environmental reports. Locally, confirm township permits, sewer/water capacity, and any municipal restrictions that affect tenant operations. Bring a civil engineer or experienced inspector for drainage, pavement, and roof assessments if the park is older. Gather zoning maps and utility letters before scheduling expensive inspections. Doing these checks early reduces surprises at closing and protects your returns. For a local due-diligence checklist and next steps, visit dmsellscre.com/investors.
