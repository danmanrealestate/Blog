Lesson 50 — Evaluating Tenant Quality and Lease Strength. Tenant quality is more than a credit score. It’s the combination of a tenant’s payment history, business resilience, guarantor strength, and whether the lease actually makes those obligations enforceable. Begin by requesting P&L statements, bank references, and proof of licenses and insurance. Audit the lease for guarantees, assignment limits, default remedies, CAM clarity, escalation formulas, and renewal deadlines. Locally, confirm zoning, parking, and peak-hour traffic in Chester and Montgomery counties; neighborhood fit can make or break a tenancy. Red flags include erratic deposits, bounced payments, ambiguous lease language, or insufficient insurance. Decision rule: if material lease ambiguities can’t be fixed before closing, treat the deal as higher risk or walk.
