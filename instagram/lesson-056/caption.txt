Rent rolls are one of the most useful documents you’ll request during commercial real estate due diligence. They list tenants, stated rents, lease start and end dates, concessions, and reported occupancy—but the numbers alone rarely tell the whole story. This lesson shows how to read a rent roll like an investor: • Request it early — it reveals income timing and rollover risk. • Reconcile each rent line to the signed lease and payment history. • Convert nominal rents to effective rents by accounting for abatements or graduated increases. • Confirm the legal payor, guarantees, CAM and tax pass-throughs. For Chester and Montgomery County investors and landlords, prioritize tenant stability in small centers and mixed-use blocks. Local market quirks can turn a tidy rent roll into a risky cash-flow forecast if you don’t verify documents. Need help interpreting a rent roll before you submit an offer?
